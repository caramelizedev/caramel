# ADR 0031: A process keeps one row per key, and its steps, compensations and deadlines are Cold Brew jobs that each change one aggregate

Date: 2026-10-09

Status: accepted.

## Context

A command changes one aggregate, and a policy reacts to one event. A domain also needs workflows that span aggregates over days and undo their work when a step is refused: tins held for an order until its parcel leaves, and given back when the order is cancelled or the parcel is late. Such a workflow needs state of its own, a timer that survives a restart, and an answer when a handler is refused.

## Decision

The words and their options are stated on the website ([site.html](../../../website/source/site.html)), chiefly its Domain-Driven Design guide, in the sections cited below by anchor, and in the doc comments under [src/caramel/sweet_talk](../../../src/caramel/sweet_talk), chiefly `process.cr` and `policy.cr`. This record states what was chosen.

1. **A process is a declared workflow with one row per key.** `process Name, means: "…" do … end` in a context keeps one row per `key`, with attributes, a lifecycle (`moves`) and deadlines (`site#c-sweettalk-processes`, `Process`, the context's `process` macro). It takes `renamed_from:` as a projection does: its table keeps the old name and its queued jobs still run, by the rename rule of [ADR 0030](0030-scope-and-modules.md) and the job-name rule of [ADR 0041](0041-events.md) (`site#c-sweettalk-renames`). It declares queries over its rows as [ADR 0035](0035-reads-and-rules.md) says. Its rows are never removed (`site#c-sweettalk-limits`).
2. **The key is the row's identity, and a row starts from its key alone.** An attribute other than the key is nilable or has a default its column takes, and the key cannot be optional; a process takes the same attribute types as an aggregate, and its writers are private. These are the attribute rules of [ADR 0039](0039-aggregates.md) (`site#c-sweettalk-processes`). `unique`, `index`, `check` and the `has` options that declare them do not compile in a process: the key's unique index is the only one, as [ADR 0045](0045-database-constraints.md) says (`site#c-sweettalk-refusals`). The row keeps an Int64 `id` whatever the domain's id type, and in a tenanted context there is one row per tenant per key, both as [ADR 0033](0033-framework-boundary.md) says.
3. **A step is a `whenever` with `key:`, run as a job on the locked row.** `whenever Event, key: expression` in a process is a step: its job inserts the row when absent, locks it and runs the block on it (`site#c-sweettalk-processes`, `Process.whenever`). The row lives in the handler's `SweetTalk::Work` on its fiber and is saved when the step returns. A command enqueues the processes' steps after the policies and the projections' handlers, and a step runs as its event's actor and in the tenant its event was emitted in; job order, job names, retries, `after:` and metadata are the rules of [ADR 0041](0041-events.md) (`site#c-sweettalk-events`, `site#c-sweettalk-metadata`), and the tenant rule is [ADR 0033](0033-framework-boundary.md)'s (`site#c-sweettalk-tenancy`).
4. **A step changes at most one aggregate, or runs one unit; its own row does not count.** The limit is the one a service has, and the unit is the step's one change, as the rule on where a unit may run in [ADR 0046](0046-transactions.md) says (`Handling`, `site#c-sweettalk-units`). `unit` in a process does not compile.
5. **A process may follow another context's events outside a border.** Doing so draws an event edge on the context map of [ADR 0036](0036-strategic-design.md) (`site#c-sweettalk-processes`).
6. **A compensation answers a refused handler.** `compensate Event do |event, …, refusal| … end`, after a `whenever` for the same event in the same context, border or process, runs when that handler is refused. The handler's work has rolled back; the compensation gets the handler's arguments and the refusal, and runs in its own transaction, in a process on the same row. It changes one aggregate or runs one unit, as a service does, and runs as its event's actor. A refusal there fails the job. A `compensate` without its `whenever`, or in a projection, does not compile (`site#c-sweettalk-processes`, `site#c-sweettalk-refusals`, `__sweettalk_compensation`).
7. **A deadline is a job scheduled for a time, and an arming counter decides whether it still runs.** `deadline name, after: span do … end` in a process: `arm name` sets `<name>_due` and enqueues a job for that time, `arm name, after: other` uses another span, and `disarm name` clears it. The job runs the block only when no arming or disarming came after its own (`Process.deadline`, `Process.arm`, `Process.disarm`, `site#c-sweettalk-processes`). `arm` of a deadline the process lacks, and `deadline` outside a process, do not compile (`site#c-sweettalk-refusals`). The Box Office tutorial arms one for a hold's expiry (`site#c-boxoffice-timer`).
8. **A deadline's block is one handling, with an actor of its own.** It acts as `deadline <Process>.<name>` (`site#c-sweettalk-metadata`), changes one aggregate or runs one unit as a step does (`Handling`), and runs in the tenant that armed it; a step or a deadline whose tenant is gone fails as [ADR 0033](0033-framework-boundary.md) says. `attempts:`, `backoff:` and `base:` on `deadline` set its job's retry rule as they do on `whenever`, after the two rules that fail a refusal and a `SweetTalk::Boundary` at once ([ADR 0041](0041-events.md), `__sweettalk_retries`). An event the block emits has as its cause the event whose step armed the deadline ([ADR 0041](0041-events.md)).

## Reasons

- One aggregate per transaction holds for compensations and process steps as it does for commands and services ([ADR 0046](0046-transactions.md)). A process's row is its own state, as a projection's is, so a step may change it and one aggregate.
- Deadlines use `run_at` and an arming counter because Cold Brew ([ADR 0009](../0009-cold-brew.md)) cannot cancel a job: a job that finds a newer arming does nothing.
- A compensation is the policy's own answer to a refusal. Without it, a parcel dispatched for a cancelled order left Ordering's job failed.
